Eliza Labs · 2025

Auto.fun

A no-code launchpad that gives every AI agent its own token — designed so creators, not snipers, win the launch.

Role
Head of Engineering — led development
Launched
17 April 2025
Chain
Solana
Built with
elizaOS · TypeScript · Raydium · Bonding curves · Liquidity NFTs
(01)

Context

Eliza Labs is the company behind elizaOS, the open-source AI-agent framework that topped GitHub’s global trending charts in early 2025. Auto.fun was its pump.fun-style launchpad: anyone could configure an autonomous agent without writing code and launch a token alongside it.

It went live on Solana on 17 April 2025, with partner projects such as FightFi, Comput3 and Sigma Music Agent on board at launch.

(02)

The challenge

Pump.fun-style launches are fast but brutal: bots snipe the first blocks, teams end up holding little of their own supply, and liquidity is fragile once trading opens. For agents meant to run for months — posting on X, trading, managing DeFi positions — that model breaks the economics before the agent has shipped.

(03)

What I did

  • Led development of Auto.fun as Head of Engineering — from the no-code agent builder on elizaOS to the token launch on Solana.
  • Introduced the “fairer than fair” token model: a hybrid bonding curve, built with Raydium, that lets a team secure up to 50% of its own supply before the token lists.
  • Paired it with liquidity NFTs backed by Raydium, so creators keep earning a share of swap fees after launch.
(04)

How it works

  1. 01BuildConfigure an agent on elizaOS — no code.
  2. 02LaunchMint the agent’s token on Solana.
  3. 03SecureThe team takes up to 50% on the bonding curve, ahead of the bots.
  4. 04TradeSOL pools on Raydium, with liquidity held as an NFT.
  5. 05EarnA share of every swap fee flows back to the creator.
(05)

Outcome

Auto.fun launched on 17 April 2025 with partner projects on day one and was covered by The Block, Cointelegraph and ForkLog. Agents started on X, with DeFi and gaming integrations on the roadmap.
